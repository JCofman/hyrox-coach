# Load Management

## Weekly structure targets

| Level        | Sessions/week  | Hours/week | Run km/week (peak) | Runs | Strength | Hyrox-specific |
| ------------ | -------------- | ---------- | ------------------ | ---- | -------- | -------------- |
| First-timer  | 3-4            | 3.5-5      | 15-25              | 2    | 1-2      | 1              |
| Intermediate | 4-5            | 5-7        | 25-40              | 2-3  | 1-2      | 1-2            |
| Advanced     | 5-7            | 7-10       | 40-60              | 3-4  | 2        | 2              |
| Competitive  | 7-10 (doubles) | 10-14      | 55-80              | 4-5  | 2-3      | 2-3            |

ESN's reference plan (developed with Hyrox world champion Luke Storath) sits at 4 × 60 min for
advanced gym athletes: a sensible default when the athlete gives no other constraint. More is
only better if they recover from it.

Start the plan at the athlete's **current** weekly load from the imported data (average of the
last 4 weeks), not at the level they aspire to.

## Progression rules

- Total hours: +10% per week at most, in 3:1 blocks (three building weeks, one recovery week
  at 60-70% volume). Masters athletes and beginners: 2:1.
- Run volume: +10-15% per week; long run +1-2 km per week; never increase run volume and add
  run intensity in the same week for run-limited athletes.
- Station loads: reach race weight by the end of the build phase; progress volume (meters, reps)
  before density (shorter rests), density before combining with running.
- At most **3 hard days per week** (intervals, heavy strength endurance, compromised/simulation).
  Never two in a row for beginners.
- Leg-heavy station work (sleds, lunges, wall balls) is run-fatigue: do not schedule it the day
  before key run intervals. Either pair them the same day (hard day hard) or separate by 48 h.

## Intensity distribution

About 75-80% of weekly training time easy (Zone 1-2, RPE ≤ 5), 20-25% at tempo and above.
Hybrid athletes habitually invert this; fixing it is often the single biggest gain.

## Session load estimate (when comparing weeks)

Use session-RPE: `load = minutes × RPE`. Works for runs, stations and strength alike and needs
no device.

| Session                    | Minutes | RPE | Load |
| -------------------------- | ------- | --- | ---- |
| Easy run                   | 45      | 4   | 180  |
| Threshold intervals        | 55      | 7   | 385  |
| Strength (max strength)    | 60      | 6   | 360  |
| Strength endurance circuit | 60      | 8   | 480  |
| Compromised running        | 65      | 8   | 520  |
| Full simulation            | 100     | 9   | 900  |

Weekly load should rise smoothly; a week more than ~30% above the 4-week average is a spike.
A full simulation is as costly as a race: treat the next 2-3 days as recovery.

## Recovery week

Every 3rd or 4th week: volume −30-40%, keep one short quality run and one moderate station
session, drop the heaviest strength work to 2 sets, no simulation. Use it for retests.

## Warning signs - adjust the plan

| Sign                                                  | Action                                                |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Resting HR +5 bpm or HRV well below baseline, 3+ days | Swap next hard session for easy or rest               |
| Easy-run HR up 8-10 bpm at the usual pace             | Recovery week now                                     |
| Shin, Achilles or knee pain that worsens on runs      | Cut running, substitute bike/erg, see a physio        |
| Low-back pain from sleds or rowing                    | Reduce load, check technique, core work               |
| Sleep, mood or motivation dropping for a week         | Take 2-3 easy days; do not "push through"             |
| Missed sessions                                       | Skip them; never stack missed work onto the next days |

## Sickness and breaks

- Symptoms above the neck only: easy training allowed. Fever or chest symptoms: no training.
- Up to 5 days off: resume where you left off. 1-2 weeks off: resume at 70% and rebuild over a
  week. 3+ weeks: step back one phase.
